The problem

MRI is not a calibrated measurement.

Scan the same person on two machines and you get two different images. Brightness, contrast, noise and fine detail all shift with the scanner. A model trained on one hospital's machines learns those quirks along with the anatomy, and its accuracy can drop when it meets a scanner it has not seen.

What changes between scanners

  • VendorSiemens, GE and Philips reconstruct images differently, before anyone sees them.
  • Field strength1.5T and 3T produce different contrast and noise for the same tissue.
  • ProtocolSequence timing, resolution and acceleration vary from site to site.
  • The machine itselfField inhomogeneity, gradient nonlinearity and drift over time.

Why physics

Scanner differences have physical causes.

Field strength, B1 inhomogeneity, gradient nonlinearity, vendor reconstruction, noise from parallel imaging. Each one changes the image in a specific way. We look for those causes instead of treating every shift as noise, which is why our reports say what went wrong rather than only how much.

Alongside client work we are building tooling for scanner-robust MRI measurement.
