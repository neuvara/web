Neuvara

Multi-site brain MRI has a measurement problem.

Scanner differences can produce variation comparable in size to the biological change studies are trying to measure.

Neuvara works on harmonisation: removing scanner-driven variance while preserving biological signal.

Who we are

Neuvara is a UK research-stage company, run by two founders working part-time.

We have no product, no customers, no deployments, and no published results yet.

The problem

Multi-site brain MRI is affected by scanner manufacturer, field strength, sequence timing, protocol, and software version. Those differences change measured volumes and image-derived features even when the underlying anatomy is unchanged.

In longitudinal studies, the signal of interest can be small. Annual brain atrophy in neurodegenerative disease is often a subtle change. A scanner change can be larger than that change.

This matters when imaging measures are used to follow change over time. If scanner variance is left in the measure, it can obscure biology. If correction removes biological signal as well, the measure may look cleaner while becoming less useful.

What we're working on

We work on harmonisation methods for multi-site brain MRI. We evaluate them against established baselines.

The main question is not only whether scanner information is reduced. It is whether biological signal survives correction.

A method that removes scanner-driven variance and biology together is easy to make. It can look tidy. It is not useful.

Direction

Where this could go

Our primary direction is prognostic imaging measures for clinical trials: measures that remain stable when a participant changes scanner, so they can be used to improve trial efficiency.

Longer term, scanner-invariant representations may support diagnostic research applications. That is a research direction, not a current capability.
